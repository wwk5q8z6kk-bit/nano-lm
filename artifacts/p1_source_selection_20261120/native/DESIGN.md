# Native Nano synthetic source-copy experiment

This exploratory experiment compares training objectives for the archived N2 hybrid HDCD, attention and causal copy-pointer model. Archived native sequence learners recovered 0 of 345 present unseen values, although all fit the decoder. The historical screen and protected H6/H7 results remain separate. The P1 charter authorizes local exploratory synthetic training, and the owner's November 20 authorization funds this bounded Google experiment. The project charter at d109d068, lines 59–69, describes the compute and privacy constraints.

Three arms share one train-only tokenizer, paired starting weights, batch indices, tensor inputs, 240 AdamW steps at 0.004, batch size 24, clipping at 1 and the same 16-token greedy decoder. All retain the numerical budget of the earlier N2 training recipe. The full-sequence arm predicts every next token. The answers-only arm supervises the final answer tokens and newline. The source-aligned arm adds an equal-weight mean negative log probability of selecting the corresponding source position and opening its existing copy gate on present answer tokens. For alignment only, choose the most recent exact whole value occurrence in the transcript. No added parameters or inference constraints. Different objective terms have different scales and extra source labels are a deliberate training intervention.

Fixed synthetic encounter seeds are 60601, 60602 and 60603 for 240 training, 48 development and 96 test encounters. Three separate invented value pools reuse the original formatting and grammar. No whole value overlaps with an archived or other split pool. Model initialization seeds 1481, 3527 and 6029 run against the same sealed encounters. No tuning, early stopping, checkpoint selection or adaptive repeats. Each seed produces predictions for the same 384 test queries. The primary measure is exact string recovery among truly present held-out values, reported by seed. Secondary counts distinguish erroneous fills on absent slots, omitted present values, wrong nonempty answers, retraction-specific failures and supersession cases. Development scores are descriptive. The task assesses these synthetic templates and vocabulary transfer. It does not establish a clinical evaluation or an independent replication across data generators.
