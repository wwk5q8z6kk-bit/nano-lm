# G04 third study (joint): a three-way router (2026-11-22)

Joint study with the memory-line session. It uses that session's two-table memory and suggestions on the gate and the measures; the ontogenesis session added the reversed-order arm and the reverse-failure measure. Protocol, development record, settings, predictions Z1–Z8 and scorer: `PROTOCOL-THREEWAY.md` and `threeway_score.py`, committed in `77e2131` before any registered run. Seeds 7301–7305. Raw files: `results/threeway/`; scored output: `results/threeway/verdict.json`. [Observed]

**Question.** Can one label-free router handle all three surprises in one world?
- exceptions to private storage;
- noise held back;
- rule changes into the shared rule.

**Answer.** Yes, in this world, and only when the change detector runs before private routing. Each detector alone fails at one of the three.

| Arm | Old entities revised after a change | New entities, 1–5 phases after | Exceptions kept | Noise resisted | Stable rule | Facts sent private |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No routing | 15% | 28% | 33% | 92% | 83% | 0 |
| Private routing only (τ = 0.1) | 11% | 22% | 52% | **50%** | 80% | 645 |
| Private routing only (τ = 0.2) | 14% | 26% | 35% | 84% | 82% | 182 |
| Change detection only | 90% | 87% | **22%** | 93% | 77% | 0 |
| **Three-way, change detector first** | **86%** | **84%** | **89%** | **91%** | 73% | 940 |
| Three-way, τ = 0.2 | 87% | 84% | 85% | 93% | 76% | 496 |
| Three-way, revision 200 steps | 43% | 52% | 99% | 92% | 79% | 761 |
| Three-way, private routing first | 43% | 50% | 91% | 91% | 75% | 948 |
| Three-way, random private choice | 87% | 85% | 84% | 92% | 75% | 924 |
| Oracle (true kinds) | 98% | 95% | 99.9% | 90% | 70% | 726 |

Means of five seeds. Three-way, change detector first: 49 flags per seed, 28 of them false, and 1.4 of 12 changes missed. Private routing first: 6.2 of 12 missed. The oracle's "missed" count is not meaningful, since it has no detector.

## What this shows

- **Each kind of surprise needs its own destination, and each single detector fails one of them.**
  - Private routing alone holds the old rule (11% revised) and absorbs noise as if it were exceptions (50% resisted).
  - Change detection alone revises well (90%) but pushes exceptions into the shared rule (22% kept).
  - Only the combination keeps all three: 86%, 89% and 91%.
- **The order of the detectors decides whether changes are learned.** With private routing first, a rule change's first instances look like confident surprises and are sent private. Six of twelve changes are then never detected, and revision halves (43%). The memory-line session predicted this pitfall; the ontogenesis session's reversed arm measured it.
- **The exemption has the predicted reverse cost.** Exceptions inside falsely flagged groups lose their private routing: 20% of them are kept, against 89% overall.
- **The stable-rule cost does not come from false alarms where they land.** Pairs hit by a false alarm keep 89%; other stable pairs fall to 70%. The cost follows the total volume of large writes: the oracle, with no false alarms but with full-type revisions and private passes, loses as much (70%). The 200-step arm, which writes less, keeps 79%. This matches the memory-line session's finding that the inference cost depends on how much is written, not which facts. It is a post-hoc reading of a failed prediction, not tested here.
- **Which new facts go private at creation matters less than expected.** A random choice of the same size keeps 84% of exceptions against 89%, because exceptions reach private storage anyway on their second sighting.
- **The revision budget is necessary.** At 200 steps, the shared write cannot overcome the type's accumulated values, and revision falls to 43%: the step-budget limit the memory-line session found for corrections.

## Limits

- **Typed addresses give this memory the types for free.** A shared revision reaches the whole type at once. Change propagation is therefore easier here than for a learner that has to discover types, such as the bilinear learner of the second study, which reached 87% with a separate detector.
- One synthetic world and five seeds. The detector's gate (older members only, threshold 0.12) was chosen on development seed 1 and is declared tuned.
- Noise and exceptions are separable by design (seen once against seen twice).
- Not a language-model result.

## Next

1. Test whether the stable-rule cost follows write volume, by matching total write steps across arms.
2. Reduce false alarms (28 per seed) without losing detection. For example, require agreement across two consecutive phases, and measure the trade-off curve.
3. Run the same router on a learner that must discover types (the bilinear model) to remove the typed-address advantage.

## Added caveat (2026-11-22): typed addresses overstate inference

The memory-line session tested data-derived addresses: entity vectors built from the facts shown at creation, not from the given types (5 seeds; protocol 25acd5d, results f22c020 on research/memory-saturation-factorial). Inference recovers only 0.27–0.34 of the gap between typed and random addresses: 0.30, against 0.74 typed and 0.11 random. Acquisition and retention are better. Greedy online type clustering collapsed on its development seed, because frozen addresses cannot revise an early wrong grouping.

The three-way studies here use typed addresses. Their inference and stable-rule figures, and the ease of propagating a rule change to a whole type, therefore hold for a memory given the types. For a learner that must discover them they are likely substantially lower. The comparisons between arms are within one learner and are not affected in direction, but their absolute levels should not be carried over.
